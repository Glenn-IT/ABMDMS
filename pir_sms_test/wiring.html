ABMDMS  ·  pir_sms_test  ·  build sheet

One sensor, one modem, one wire at a time

The smallest rig that proves the whole idea: a single PIR sensor on Pin 2 that texts your phone through a SIM800L and writes every event into MySQL. Build this before the four-room version — when something breaks here, there is only one of everything to check.

01What you need on the desk

Arduino Uno ×1 · powered over USB

The USB cable carries both power and the serial link the dashboard reads.

SIM800L V2.2 (UNV) ×1 · the 5 V board

The blue board with a 5Vin pin. It is 5 V-logic, so its RX needs no voltage divider.

HC-SR501 PIR ×1 · zone ROOM1

Three pins: VCC, OUT, GND. The order under the dome varies between batches — read yours.

1000 µF capacitor ×1 · 16 V or higher

Sits right beside the module. Without it, transmitting browns the module out mid-send.

5 V supply, 2 A or more ×1 · wall adapter preferred

For the module only. Many power banks switch themselves off at the module's idle current.

Breadboard + jumpers 830-point · M-M and M-F

Male-to-female jumpers for the module and sensor headers; male-to-male for the rails.

02Every connection, once

Eight wires and three jumpers. The three in the warning column are the ones that destroy hardware or waste an evening, so they are called out rather than buried.

FromToWhy / watch out
PIR OUT Arduino Pin 2 Goes straight to the pin, never through a rail.
PIR VCC Arduino 5V (via bottom + rail) The sensor draws almost nothing — the Arduino can feed it happily.
PIR GND common ground Bottom − rail.
SIM800L TXD Arduino Pin 10 Direct. TX talks to RX — if the module never answers AT, suspect these two are swapped.
SIM800L RXD Arduino Pin 11 Direct — V2.2 is a 5 V-logic board. No 1k/2k divider. The divider belongs to the bare 3.7 V module, not this one.
SIM800L RST Arduino Pin 12 Optional but recommended — the sketch pulses it to recover a stuck module.
SIM800L 5Vin external supply + External only. Transmit bursts pull about 2 A; the Arduino's regulator gives roughly 0.5 A and browns out.
SIM800L GND supply − and Arduino GND Must be one shared net, or serial cannot work at all.
SIM800L VDD nothing Leave unconnected. It is a 2.8 V reference output, not a power input — feeding it 5 V damages the board.
1000 µF + module 5Vin In parallel across the module's power pins, not in series.
1000 µF − (stripe) module GND Polarity matters. Backwards, it heats up and vents.

Filled dots are connections. The capacitor hangs across the module's own power pins, as close to them as the wires allow — put it at the far end of a long rail and the wire resistance between cancels most of what it is there to do.

03The four rails, and who owns them

This build has two separate 5 V supplies that must never meet, and one ground shared by everything. Getting that right is most of the wiring.

TOP + rail external supply only

Wall adapter → the SIM800L's 5Vin. The Arduino's 5 V pin never touches this rail.

TOP − rail common ground

Supply −, module GND, and — through the bridge jumper — the Arduino's ground.

BOTTOM − rail common ground

Arduino GND and the PIR's ground. Bridged to the top − rail so it is all one net.

BOTTOM + rail arduino 5 V — pir only

Feeds the one HC-SR501. Nothing else belongs on it.

The one rule

Bridge the two − rails. Never bridge the two + rails. Ground must be one continuous net or the serial link cannot work at all. The two 5 V rails come from different supplies — joining them back-feeds the Arduino's regulator and puts the module's 2 A transmit burst onto a rail that can give 0.5 A.

Most full-size boards also split each rail in the middle — look for a break in the red and blue lines near column 30. If two wires sit on opposite sides of that break they are not connected; bridge the halves of the rail you are using.

04The breadboard, hole by hole

Drawn as a full-size 830-point board: 63 columns, rows A–J, four power rails. The column numbers are a suggestion; the rail each wire lands in is not. On a half-size 400-point board, keep the rails and shift the columns down to fit.

The small semicircular bumps are wire hops: that wire passes over the rail it arches across and does not connect to it. Only the filled dots are connections.

05Check the board before you power it

01Set the multimeter to continuity. Probe the two + rails — it must not beep. If it does, you have bridged them somewhere.
02Probe the two − rails — it must beep. That is the bridge jumper doing its job.
03Probe each rail end to end. Silence in the middle means your board's rails are split at column 30 — bridge the halves.
04Probe the module's 5Vin wire against the Arduino's 5 V pin — it must not beep.
05Check the capacitor is across two different rails, not two holes in the same one. Same rail = a short across your supply.
06Confirm the stripe leg of the capacitor is the one in the − rail. Backwards, it vents.
07Confirm VDD on the module has nothing plugged into it at all.
08Screw the antenna on before powering up. Transmitting without one damages the radio.

06Power up and read the status LED

Power and antenna first, before you upload anything. The blink rate is the module telling you whether it found a 2G network — nothing after this can work until this does.

one blink · every ~3 s
joined the network

Registered on 2G. Upload pir_sms.ino and go on.

one blink · every ~1 s
still searching

No antenna, no coverage, or a SIM problem. Test the SIM in a phone forced to 2G, in this same room.

nothing, or on and off in a loop
power problem

Check the capacitor's polarity, then the supply. Power banks often cut out at the module's low idle current.

07What the Arduino says, and where it goes

Two paths from one wave of the hand

PIR → Arduino → SIM800L → your phone
PIR → Arduino → USB → serial_reader.ps1 → PHP API → MySQL → dashboard

The text and the database entry are independent. If the SMS fails, the motion is still recorded — and the failure is recorded too, which is exactly what makes this rig worth building.

08When it doesn't work

SymptomMost likely causeFix
Module never answers AT — SIM_FAIL:NOREPLY TXD and RXD swapped, or grounds not shared Swap pins 10 and 11. Confirm the − rails are bridged.
Module restarts mid-send Brownout on the 2 A transmit burst Capacitor closer to the module; short thick wires for 5Vin/GND; a wall adapter, not a bank.
First text sends, then TIMEOUT followed by ERROR, over and over Brownout, confirmed. The TIMEOUT is the module dying mid-send; it reboots, which forgets AT+CMGF=1, so the next send gets ERROR straight away. Fix the power — no sketch change cures this. Watch the status LED: if it goes dark and re-blinks after each attempt, the module is resetting.
Signal: prints garbage like )-5?AT+CSQ Command echo is on, so the module's repeat of your own question is read as the answer Send ATE0 during start-up. The sketch does this from the start.
Module switches on and off in a loop Capacitor in backwards Stripe leg goes to GND. Replace the capacitor if it got hot.
Power bank shuts itself off after a minute Idle draw is under the bank's auto-cutoff Add a 47 Ω 1 W bleeder across the supply, or use a wall adapter.
PIR fires constantly right after power-on Still in its 30-second warm-up Stay out of its view until System Ready prints.
Reader says "Cannot reach the API" Apache is not running Start Apache in the XAMPP Control Panel.
Reader cannot open COM5 The Arduino IDE Serial Monitor still holds the port Close the Serial Monitor. Only one program can hold a COM port.
Dashboard stays empty but the reader shows [OK] The SQL was never imported, or MySQL is stopped Import database/pir_sms_test.sql in phpMyAdmin.
