ABMDMS  ·  build sheet

Three rooms, one modem, one wire at a time

Three PIR sensors on Arduino pins 2–4, each watching its own room, all texting through a single SIM800L and writing into MySQL. The single-sensor rig proved the chain works; this sheet is the same build with more sensors hung off the same two power rails. Add them one at a time — section 05 says why.

Pin 5 / Room D is retired

This sheet used to describe a fourth sensor on Pin 5, zone ROOMD. During bring-up that pin would not respond to two different sensors, so Room D was taken out of the system and the drawings below now show three.

Two sensors failing on the same pin points at the pin, the OUT wire, or that sensor's rail tap — not the sensors. To chase it, move the suspect sensor to pin 6 or 7 and retest; if it works there, Pin 5 or its wiring is the fault. Restoring Room D afterwards is four small edits, listed in README.md.

01What you need on the desk

Arduino Uno ×1 · powered over USB

The USB cable carries both power and the serial link the dashboard reads.

SIM800L V2.2 (UNV) ×1 · the 5 V board

The blue board with a 5Vin pin. It is 5 V-logic, so its RX needs no voltage divider.

HC-SR501 PIR ×3 · ROOMA / ROOMB / ROOMC

Three pins: VCC, OUT, GND. The order under the dome varies between batches — read each one.

1000 µF capacitor ×1 · 16 V or higher

Sits right beside the module. Without it, transmitting browns the module out mid-send.

5 V supply, 2 A or more ×1 · wall adapter preferred

For the module only. Many power banks switch themselves off at the module's idle current.

Breadboard + jumpers 830-point · 15× M-F, 8× M-M

Male-to-female for the module and the twelve sensor pins; male-to-male for the rails and the ground bridge.

02Every connection, once

Twenty wires. Adding three sensors adds nine of them and changes nothing else — the SIM800L half is untouched from the one-sensor build. The rows in the warning colour are the ones that destroy hardware or waste an evening, so they are called out rather than buried.

The capacitor still belongs beside the module's own power pins — put it at the far end of a long rail and the wire resistance between cancels most of what it is there to do.

03The four rails, and who owns them

This build has two separate 5 V supplies that must never meet, and one ground shared by everything. Getting that right is most of the wiring.

TOP + rail external supply only

Wall adapter → the SIM800L's 5Vin. The Arduino's 5 V pin never touches this rail.

TOP − rail common ground

Supply −, module GND, and — through the bridge jumper — the Arduino's ground.

BOTTOM − rail common ground

Arduino GND and all three PIR grounds. Bridged to the top − rail so it is all one net.

BOTTOM + rail arduino 5 V — pirs only

Feeds all three HC-SR501s. Nothing else belongs on it — especially not the module.

Adding sensors does not add a rail. All three VCC pins land in the same bottom + rail and all three GND pins in the same bottom − rail, just in different columns. The only thing each sensor keeps to itself is its OUT wire.

The one rule

Bridge the two − rails. Never bridge the two + rails. Ground must be one continuous net or the serial link cannot work at all. The two 5 V rails come from different supplies — joining them back-feeds the Arduino's regulator and puts the module's 2 A transmit burst onto a rail that can give 0.5 A.

Most full-size boards also split each rail in the middle — look for a break in the red and blue lines near column 30. If two wires sit on opposite sides of that break they are not connected; bridge the halves of the rail you are using.

04The breadboard, hole by hole

Drawn as a full-size 830-point board: 63 columns, rows A–J, four power rails. The column numbers are a suggestion; the rail each wire lands in is not. On a half-size 400-point board, keep the rails and shift the columns down to fit. Spread the sensors out as shown — not because the electricity cares, but because you will be probing and re-seating these wires, and crowded taps are what turn a loose OUT wire into an hour of guessing.

The small semicircular bumps are wire hops: that wire passes over the rail it arches across and does not connect to it. Only the filled dots are connections. The three data jumpers for the SIM800L (TXD→10, RXD→11, RST→12) go module-header to Arduino-header and skip the breadboard entirely, the same as the OUT wires.

05Set each sensor, then add them one at a time

Every HC-SR501 has two adjustments and a jumper, and they ship at random settings. Do them all before anything is wired, while you can still turn them over easily.

01Move the yellow jumper to H (repeat-trigger). On L the sensor goes deaf for its whole delay period after each trigger, so someone still in the room reads as gone.
02Turn the time-delay screw fully anti-clockwise — the shortest hold. The sketch decides when motion has stopped; the sensor holding its output high for minutes only hides that.
03Leave the sensitivity screw mid-range to start. Turn it down later if a sensor keeps firing at nothing.
04Read the V / O / G letters printed under each dome. The order differs between batches — check each one separately, they may not agree with each other.

Now build up in stages. Wire one sensor, upload the sketch, open the Serial Monitor at 9600, and confirm that waving at it prints the room you expect — not just a room. Only then add the next. Several sensors wired all at once give you a pile of possible pin-to-room mix-ups and no way to tell which one you have.

If a sensor fires with nobody near it

Unplug that sensor's OUT wire from the Arduino and jumper that pin straight to GND instead. If the phantom triggers stop, the sketch is fine and that sensor's OUT wire is not seated properly — a floating input picks up whatever noise is nearby and reports it as a person. If they continue, the problem is in the sketch or that pin.

This is the fastest test in the whole build and it is worth doing before you suspect anything else. Add sensors one at a time and it also tells you which sensor is the bad one.

06Check the board before you power it

01Set the multimeter to continuity. Probe the two + rails — it must not beep. If it does, you have bridged them somewhere.
02Probe the two − rails — it must beep. That is the bridge jumper doing its job.
03Probe each rail end to end. Silence in the middle means your board's rails are split at column 30 — bridge the halves. With the sensors spread from column 18 to 46 this now matters: a split rail can leave one of them dead.
04Probe the module's 5Vin wire against the Arduino's 5 V pin — it must not beep.
05Probe each OUT wire against every other — none may beep. A beep means two rooms are shorted together and will always report as one.
06Count the OUT wires landing in the Arduino header: exactly one each in pins 2, 3, 4 and 5, and nothing in any other digital pin.
07Check the capacitor is across two different rails, not two holes in the same one. Same rail = a short across your supply.
08Confirm the stripe leg of the capacitor is the one in the − rail. Backwards, it vents.
09Confirm VDD on the module has nothing plugged into it at all.
10Screw the antenna on before powering up. Transmitting without one damages the radio.

07Power up and read the status LED

Power and antenna first, before you upload anything. The blink rate is the module telling you whether it found a 2G network — nothing after this can work until this does.

one blink · every ~3 s
joined the network

Registered on 2G. Upload motion_sensor.ino and go on.

one blink · every ~1 s
still searching

No antenna, no coverage, or a SIM problem. Test the SIM in a phone forced to 2G, in this same room.

nothing, or on and off in a loop
power problem

Check the capacitor's polarity, then the supply. Power banks often cut out at the module's low idle current.

08What the Arduino says, and where it goes

Two paths from one wave of the hand

PIR → Arduino → SIM800L → your phone
PIR → Arduino → USB → serial_reader.ps1 → PHP API → MySQL → dashboard

The text and the database entry are independent. If the SMS fails, the motion is still recorded — and the failure is recorded too, which is exactly what makes this rig worth building.

Every line below carries its room in the name, and that name is the only thing telling the dashboard which sensor spoke. ROOMx here stands for any of ROOMA, ROOMB, ROOMC.

The cooldown is counted per room, not for the rig as a whole. Somebody pacing around Room A gets you one text a minute; somebody walking from Room A into Room B gets you a second text immediately. Only one message can be in flight at a time, so a second room's alert queues for a few seconds rather than being lost.

Anything else the sketch prints — the pin-to-room list at start-up, the warm-up countdown, System Ready, the signal reading — appears in the reader window but is deliberately not saved.

09When it doesn't work

SymptomMost likely causeFix
Module never answers AT — SIM_FAIL:NOREPLY TXD and RXD swapped, or grounds not shared Swap pins 10 and 11. Confirm the − rails are bridged.
Module restarts mid-send Brownout on the 2 A transmit burst Capacitor closer to the module; short thick wires for 5Vin/GND; a wall adapter, not a bank.
First text sends, then TIMEOUT followed by ERROR, over and over Brownout, confirmed. The TIMEOUT is the module dying mid-send; it reboots, which forgets AT+CMGF=1, so the next send gets ERROR straight away. Fix the power — no sketch change cures this. Watch the status LED: if it goes dark and re-blinks after each attempt, the module is resetting.
Signal: prints garbage like )-5?AT+CSQ Command echo is on, so the module's repeat of your own question is read as the answer Send ATE0 during start-up. The sketch does this from the start.
Module switches on and off in a loop Capacitor in backwards Stripe leg goes to GND. Replace the capacitor if it got hot.
Power bank shuts itself off after a minute Idle draw is under the bank's auto-cutoff Add a 47 Ω 1 W bleeder across the supply, or use a wall adapter.
A PIR fires constantly right after power-on Still in the 30-second warm-up They all warm up together — stay out of every sensor's view until System Ready prints.
Waving at Room A lights up Room B on the dashboard Two OUT wires swapped between pins Read the pin-to-room list the sketch prints at start-up, then move the wire — not the code. If you would rather move the code, edit ZONE_NAME[] in motion_sensor.ino, keeping it in pin order 2, 3, 4, 5.
One room never reports anything, the other three are fine That sensor's OUT wire is in the wrong hole, or its VCC/GND tap missed the rail Jumper that Arduino pin straight to GND: if the room still never appears, the wire is the problem. Then check its two rail taps — a tap one row off the rail looks identical and does nothing.
A pin stays dead even after swapping in a known-good sensor The pin, its OUT wire, or that position's rail taps — not the sensor Move that sensor to a spare pin (6 or 7) and retest with pir_sms_test/arduino/one_pin_test/one_pin_test.ino. If it works there, the original pin or its wiring is the fault. This is what happened to Pin 5 / Room D on this build.
Every room triggers when you wave at just one Nothing is broken — the sensors all see the same room On a bench they sit centimetres apart with ~110° cones metres deep. Cover the ones you are not testing, point them apart, or turn the sensitivity down. Confirm with pin_monitor.ino.
One room reports every time another room sends a text — but pin_monitor.ino showed clean isolation The GSM transmit burst. It pulls ~2 A for a few milliseconds and radiates hard from the antenna; PIR sensors pick up both. pin_monitor.ino cannot show this because it never uses the module. Confirm by setting SIM_ENABLED = false and re-testing — phantom rooms should vanish. Then: move the antenna away from the sensors (usually the whole fix), shorten the module's 5Vin/GND leads, keep OUT wires short, and add 0.1 µF across each PIR's VCC/GND. The sketch's START_CONFIRM_MS filter already rejects short pulses.
Two rooms always trigger together Their OUT wires are shorted, or both are in the same pin Continuity-test the two OUT wires against each other. They must not beep.
Every room reports at once, constantly The bottom − rail is not reaching the Arduino's GND, so every input floats Check the ground bridge at column 62, and that the Arduino's GND wire is in the − rail and not the + rail.
Some rooms work, others never do — and they alternate along the board The bottom rails are split in the middle and only one half is fed Bridge the two halves of both bottom rails near column 30.
Reader says "Cannot reach the API" Apache is not running Start Apache in the XAMPP Control Panel.
Reader cannot open COM5 The Arduino IDE Serial Monitor still holds the port Close the Serial Monitor. Only one program can hold a COM port.
Dashboard stays empty but the reader shows [OK] The SQL was never imported, or MySQL is stopped Import database/database.sql in phpMyAdmin.
