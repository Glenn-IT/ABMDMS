ESP32 Wireless Motion Detection & Audio Alarm Wiring Guide

Complete wiring specification featuring ESP32 Wi-Fi, SIM800L GSM, and the newly integrated MAX98357A I2S 3W Class-D Audio Amplifier & Speaker.

System Operational Flow • Wi-Fi + GSM + Loud Alarm

The ESP32 runs standalone powered by the 5V HW-131 breadboard adapter. When motion is detected, it simultaneously plays a high-power non-blocking audio siren over the MAX98357A I2S Speaker, sends an emergency alert via SIM800L GSM (UART2), and dispatches wireless HTTP POST telemetry directly to your XAMPP backend (record_motion.php & record_sms.php).

01What you need on the desk

ESP32 Dev Module ×1 · NodeMCU-32S / ESP32-WROOM

Main wireless microcontroller. Runs direct Wi-Fi HTTP client, Hardware UART2, and hardware I2S DMA audio driver.

MAX98357A I2S Amplifier ×1 · 3W Mono Class-D

Digital I2S audio DAC & Amp. Connects to ESP32 GPIOs 25 (LRC), 26 (BCLK), 27 (DIN). Powered from 5V rail.

Speaker (4Ω or 8Ω) ×1 · 3 Watt rating

Connects directly to the amplifier SPK+ and SPK- screw terminals. Delivers loud arming chirps and sweep sirens.

HW-131 Power Module ×1 · MB-102 compatible

Plugs onto breadboard end. Sets both top & bottom rails to 5V. Powers ESP32 VIN, SIM800L, PIRs, and MAX98357A.

12 V DC Wall Adapter ×1 · 1 A to 2 A rating

Plugs into HW-131 DC barrel jack (5.5mm × 2.1mm center positive). Delivers robust power during 2A GSM & 3W audio bursts.

SIM800L V2.2 (UNV) ×1 · 5 V board

Communicates with ESP32 via Hardware UART2 (GPIO 16 RX2 / GPIO 17 TX2 / GPIO 4 RST). Sends SMS text alerts.

HC-SR501 PIR Sensors ×3 · ROOMA / ROOMB / ROOMC

Powered by 5V rail. Sensor OUT provides 3.3V logic — 100% safe to plug directly into ESP32 GPIOs 12, 14, and 13.

1000 µF Capacitor ×1 · 16 V or higher

Electrolytic capacitor sitting in parallel across SIM800L 5Vin and GND to buffer 2A cellular transmit surges.

Breadboard + Jumpers 830-point · M-M & M-F

Full size 830-tie point solderless breadboard with jumper wires for modules and power rail distribution.

03The breadboard, hole by hole

Physical wiring on a full-size 830-tie point breadboard. The HW-131 module slots into columns 1–4 on the left edge, powered by the 12V DC wall adapter. The top rail provides dedicated high-current 5V for the SIM800L, while the bottom rail powers the ESP32, PIR sensors, and the MAX98357A audio amplifier.

Amplifier & Speaker Breadboard Rules

1. Never ground the speaker negative terminal: The MAX98357A uses Bridge-Tied Load (BTL) drive. Its SPK+ and SPK− outputs both carry live audio signals. Connecting SPK− to GND will short-circuit the output stage and overheat the amplifier!

2. Keep speaker wires away from the SIM800L antenna: GSM transmit bursts can induce RF ticking into speaker cables. Twist the speaker wire pair and keep them at least 10 cm away from the SIM800L antenna.

04Speaker, Amplifier & Siren Audio Behavior

The firmware uses the ESP32 hardware I2S DMA audio controller to synthesize clean, loud sine-wave alarm sweeps and system status chimes directly without blocking any sensor monitoring or Wi-Fi operations.

Audio EventAcoustic Sound ProfileFirmware Trigger in esp32_motion_sensor.ino
Boot / Arming Chime Dual rising chirp: 1600 Hz (80 ms) followed by 2400 Hz (120 ms). Fires in setup() once the 30-second PIR sensor warm-up countdown completes and Wi-Fi connects.
Motion Alarm Siren Rapid 12-sweep emergency siren: sweeps continuously from 600 Hz up to 2200 Hz over 3000 ms (3 seconds). Fires inside loop() whenever any active PIR zone confirms continuous movement for >500 ms.
Non-Blocking Playback Zero delay. DMA buffer transfers audio frames in background while the CPU continues polling sensors and Wi-Fi. Handled via speakerTick() in the main loop using i2s_write().

Step-by-Step Audio Verification Test

01Plug the 12V adapter into HW-131 and verify the green LED on the HW-131 lights up.
02Watch the Arduino Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. Confirm you see: [I2S] Audio amplifier initialized (LRC:25, BCK:26, DIN:27).
03Wait for the 30-second sensor countdown to end. Listen for the crisp two-tone arming chirp ("pip-peep!").
04Wave your hand across PIR 1, 2, or 3. Confirm the 12-cycle sweep siren (3 seconds) sounds loudly and clearly through the speaker.
05Verify the web dashboard updates instantly to show the triggered zone with a new log entry.

05Wireless Data Flow (Before vs. After)

Direct Wi-Fi Transmission • No Serial Bridge Required

• MAX98357A I2S Audio Amp → 4Ω/8Ω 3W Speaker Siren (Instant Local Deterrent)
• SIM800L (Hardware UART2) → Cellular Network → Mobile Phone (SMS Alert)

The ESP32 completely eliminates the need for USB serial tethering cables, serial_reader.ps1, or running background PowerShell scripts on your PC.

06Pre-Power Checklist

01Check the HW-131 jumpers — verify both top and bottom jumpers are seated across the 5V pins (not 3.3V or OFF).
02Multimeter continuity test: probe the top and bottom − rails — verify they beep together (confirming common ground bridge at col 60).
03Audio Amplifier Safety: verify neither SPK+ nor SPK− is connected to breadboard ground or 5V. They must connect only to the two speaker terminals.
04Check the 1000 µF capacitor polarity across the SIM800L power rails — striped leg must be seated in the − rail.
05Confirm VDD on the SIM800L is unconnected (it is an internal 2.8V reference output — feeding it 5V will burn the SIM800L radio).
06Screw the GSM antenna securely onto the SIM800L module before powering up. Transmitting without an antenna can damage the RF PA.
07Verify Wi-Fi credentials (WIFI_SSID, WIFI_PASSWORD) and your PC's IP (SERVER_IP) in esp32_motion_sensor.ino.

07Troubleshooting

SymptomProbable CauseResolution
No sound from speaker on boot or motion I2S pin mismatch or amplifier power missing Confirm LRC → GPIO 25, BCLK → GPIO 26, DIN → GPIO 27. Ensure amp VIN is wired to 5V rail and GND to common ground. Check that SPEAKER_ENABLED = true in the sketch.
Audio is distorted or speaker crackles Volume clipping or weak 5V rail Lower SIREN_VOLUME in sketch (e.g. from 28000 to 18000 or 14000). Ensure the HW-131 is powered by a 12V 1A–2A adapter rather than USB power.
Buzzing / static clicking in speaker during SMS GSM RF transmission coupling into audio wires Twist the two speaker wires tightly together. Route the speaker and amplifier wires at least 15 cm away from the SIM800L GSM antenna.
ESP32 reboots when alarm siren plays Voltage drop on 5V rail caused by simultaneous audio & GSM draw Ensure the 1000 µF capacitor is installed on the power rails. Use a 12V 2A DC wall adapter. Optionally add a 470 µF capacitor across the amp VIN and GND pins.
Wi-Fi connection fails (stuck on connecting dots) 5GHz network or incorrect credentials ESP32 only supports 2.4GHz Wi-Fi. Verify your Wi-Fi SSID and password in esp32_motion_sensor.ino.
HTTP POST returns error (-1 or Connection Refused) Wrong PC IP or Windows Firewall blocking Port 80 Find your PC's IPv4 via ipconfig in Command Prompt and update SERVER_IP. Ensure Apache is running in XAMPP and allowed in Windows Firewall.
SIM800L no reply to AT commands GPIO 16/17 swapped or power insufficient Ensure SIM800L TXD → GPIO 16 (RX2) and RXD → GPIO 17 (TX2). Verify HW-131 power switch is pushed in (ON).
