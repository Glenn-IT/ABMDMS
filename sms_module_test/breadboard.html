The breadboard, hole by hole

Drawn as a full-size 830-point board — 63 columns, rows A–J, four power rails. Column numbers are a suggestion; the rail each wire lands in is not. If yours is a half-size 400-point board, use the same rails and shift the columns down to fit.

01The four rails, and who owns them

The HW-131 Breadboard Power Module (fed by a 12 V wall adapter) powers both the top and bottom breadboard rails with clean, regulated 5 V, while Ground is shared across everything.

TOP + rail HW-131 5 V output

Powers the SIM800L's 5Vin. Jumper set to 5V. Buffered by 1000µF capacitor.

TOP − rail common ground

HW-131 −, module GND, and Arduino ground.

BOTTOM − rail common ground

Arduino GND and all PIR grounds. Connected to top − rail via HW-131 / bridge.

BOTTOM + rail HW-131 5 V output

Powers the PIR sensors. Jumper set to 5V. (Unused during SIM800L bench test).

Power Connection Rule

HW-131 supplies 5V to Arduino 5V pin & rails. Share the GND line. The HW-131 powers both rails directly from the 12 V wall adapter. The Arduino connects over USB for serial data communication with the PC.

Most full-size boards also split each rail in the middle (column 30). Bridge the rail halves with short jumpers so power reaches all columns.

04Check the board before you power it

01Check the HW-131 jumpers — confirm both top and bottom jumpers are set to 5V (not 3.3V or OFF).
02Probe the two − rails with continuity mode — it must beep (shared ground).
03Probe each rail end to end. Silence in the middle means your board's rails are split at column 30 — bridge the halves.
04Confirm the Arduino's 5V pin is completely unconnected.
05Check the capacitor is across two different rails (+ and −), not two holes in the same one.
06Confirm the stripe leg of the capacitor is in the − rail. Backwards, it vents.
