The breadboard, hole by hole

Drawn as a full-size 830-point board — 63 columns, rows A–J, four power rails. Column numbers are a suggestion; the rail each wire lands in is not. If yours is a half-size 400-point board, use the same rails and shift the columns down to fit.

01The four rails, and who owns them

This build has two separate 5 V supplies that must never meet, and one ground that must be shared by everything. Getting that right is most of the wiring.

TOP + rail external supply only

Power bank / 5 V 2 A adapter → the SIM800L's 5Vin. The Arduino's 5 V pin never touches this rail.

TOP − rail common ground

Supply −, module GND, and — through the bridge jumper — the Arduino's ground.

BOTTOM − rail common ground

Arduino GND and the four PIR grounds. Bridged to the top − rail so it is all one net.

BOTTOM + rail arduino 5 V — pirs only

Feeds the four HC-SR501 sensors. Unused during the SMS bench test.

The one rule

Bridge the two − rails. Never bridge the two + rails. Ground must be one continuous net or the serial link cannot work at all. The two 5 V rails come from different supplies — joining them back-feeds the Arduino's regulator from the power bank, and puts the module's 2 A transmit burst onto a rail that can give 0.5 A.

Most full-size boards also split each rail in the middle (look for a break in the red and blue lines near column 30). If your wires sit on opposite sides of that break they are not connected — bridge the two halves of the rail you are using.

02Bench-test layout — SMS module only

This is the rig for Tests B and C: no PIR sensors, no Arduino 5 V. Only the right-hand end of the board is used, because the capacitor has to sit near the module where the current burst happens.

The little semicircular bumps are wire hops: that wire passes over the rail it arches across and does not connect to it. Only the filled dots are connections.

04Check the board before you power it

01Set the multimeter to continuity. Probe the two + rails — it must not beep. If it does, you have bridged them somewhere.
02Probe the two − rails — it must beep. That is the bridge jumper doing its job.
03Probe each rail end to end. Silence in the middle means your board's rails are split at column 30 — bridge the halves.
04Probe the module's 5Vin wire against the Arduino's 5 V pin — it must not beep.
05Check the capacitor is across two different rails, not two holes in the same one. Same rail = a short across your supply.
06Confirm the stripe leg of the capacitor is the one in the − rail. Backwards, it vents.
